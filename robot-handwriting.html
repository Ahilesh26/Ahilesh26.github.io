Robot Handwriting for Sign Language

This project sits at a cross-section of problems I find genuinely compelling: accessibility, gesture recognition, and physical robot output. The premise — build a system that watches a person signing in American Sign Language, identifies the letter they're forming, and instructs a robot arm to write that letter on paper — is straightforward. Building it required solving non-trivial problems at every layer: perception, classification, trajectory generation, and motion control.

ASL finger-spelling is the tractable subset of sign language recognition: each letter corresponds to a specific hand configuration, the camera can capture them from a fixed viewpoint, and the classification task is bounded to 26 classes.

Detection Pipeline

MediaPipe Hands extracts 21 3D landmarks per frame. From these I compute a feature vector of inter-joint angles and normalised distances, stripping out global hand position and scale so the classifier is invariant to where the hand sits in frame. The classifier is a small MLP trained on roughly 200 samples per class, captured across multiple lighting conditions and hand sizes. Inference runs at the full frame rate with compute to spare for downstream robot control.

From Detection to Handwriting

Once a letter is confidently classified — held stable for 1 second to avoid false triggers — the system looks up a pre-defined stroke trajectory for that letter. Each letter is stored as a sequence of Cartesian waypoints in the writing plane, parameterised along arc length. The robot arm interpolates between these using a cubic spline, giving smooth natural-looking strokes. Pen-up and pen-down commands coordinate with the trajectory at a fixed writing speed of 8mm/s along the stroke.

What surprised me most was how much the handwriting improved when I switched from linear waypoint interpolation to cubic splines. The letter forms went from looking like they were drawn with a ruler to something that actually resembled natural handwriting — and all I changed was the interpolation method.
