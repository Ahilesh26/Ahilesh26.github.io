Robotics & Automation Engineer

Ahilesh Rajaram

Robotics & Automation  ·  Mechatronics  ·  Wearable Systems

I build robots and the systems that control them — from concept and simulation through to physical hardware that actually works in the real world. My interest is in the gap between the clean theory and the messy reality: the constraints you only discover when something is bolted together and switched on.

When I'm not in the lab or the workshop I'm on the basketball court. High performance in both fields comes down to the same thing: rigorous preparation, fast iteration, and the composure to stay systematic under pressure.

I design and commission PLC-driven control architectures for heavy-scale manufacturing — working across Allen-Bradley and Siemens platforms to build Ladder Logic programs, configure HMI/SCADA systems, and integrate industrial networks using protocols like OPC-UA. On the factory floor I've learned how automation decisions cascade: a poorly-tuned loop or misconfigured network segment can affect an entire production line. I approach these systems with a focus on reliability, diagnostics, and long-term maintainability over short-term cleverness.

My mechatronics work lives at the intersection of mechanical design, embedded systems, and software. I develop autonomous robotic workcells using ROS 2 — integrating perception pipelines, motion planning with MoveIt 2, and real hardware including UR5e arms, Fanuc, and Denso controllers. I've validated systems in simulation before deploying to hardware, and I care about the full stack: from the CAD geometry of an end-effector to the control law running on it. The goal is always a system that handles the unexpected gracefully, not just the nominal case cleanly.

My wearable robotics work is driven by a straightforward belief: assistive technology should conform to the human body, not the other way around. I've designed and validated passive exoskeletons, bionic prosthetics, and assistive feeding systems — using SolidWorks and Abaqus for structural analysis and OpenSim and Mokka for biomechanical modelling. Safety and physical human-robot interaction are central to everything in this space. I use impedance and admittance control frameworks because they let the robot respond to the person rather than imposing fixed trajectories on them.
